## The question

Computing education borrows its learning theories from elsewhere — from psychology, from
maths education, from the learning sciences generally. Borrowed well, they explain a great
deal. Borrowed loosely, they turn into vocabulary.

Which theories does the field actually use, and can they be organised so that a researcher
or a teacher can see what applies where?

## What I made

A contribution to a short paper in the ITiCSE 2019 proceedings, written with nine other
people across six countries — researchers in Australia, Canada, Finland, Ireland, the UK
and the United States.

I was the only author not at a university. ==That is the part worth noting: the collaboration
put someone whose job is making classroom resources in the same room as people who study
whether they work, which is the relationship the rest of this site keeps circling.==

## What I did myself

Contributed as one of ten. I am not going to inflate a two-page paper into more than it was
— the value to me was in the collaboration and in learning how researchers argue about this
material, rather than in a byline.
