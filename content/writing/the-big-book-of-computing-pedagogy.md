## The question

By this point there were a good number of [[pedagogy-quick-reads|Quick Reads]], some
reports, work on progression, and a set of pedagogical principles we had devised and
published through the National Centre for Computing Education.

All of it was out there, and all of it was scattered. Could it be assembled into one thing
that a teacher would keep — and could that thing reach beyond the audience the NCCE work
was already reaching?

## What I made

A book with a chapter for each of the twelve principles of computing pedagogy: lead with
concepts, structure lessons, make concrete, unplug–unpack–repack, work together, read and
explore code first, foster program comprehension, model everything, challenge
misconceptions, create projects, get hands-on, add variety.

Each chapter connects the principle to research and then to practice, deliberately mixing
three kinds of article: research-based pieces, pieces that sit between research and
practice, and pieces written straight from the classroom. Some of it came from past issues
of _Hello World_; the rest was commissioned for the book.

That mix is the argument. A principle stated alone is a slogan. A principle with the
research behind it and a teacher's account of using it on a Tuesday is something you can
act on.

## What I did myself

The concept was not mine — the Quick Reads were, this wasn't. I'm credited in the book as
its **pedagogical consultant**, and that is the honest description: I did the pedagogical
work rather than owning the idea.

I worked in a small team — my manager, our Chief Learning Officer, and the _Hello World_
editor — to pull the thing together.

## Where it went

Given away to subscribers first, then several further print runs. Given away at
conferences, and sold to ship overseas for the people we couldn't post to for free.

The reaction was well beyond anything I expected. People keep it on their shelves and call
it their computing pedagogy bible. Reading groups have formed that work through the
chapters as a way of discussing pedagogy. People from all over the world have told us how
they use it, either for their own teaching or with the teachers they serve.

It is the piece of work I am most proud of.
